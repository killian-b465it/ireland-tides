The Ultimate Beginner's Guide to Sea Fishing in Ireland

By Killian, Irish Fishing Hub • March 25, 2026 • 7 Min Read

Ireland boasts over 3,000 kilometers of rugged, beautiful coastline, jutting out into the rich, nutrient-dense waters of the Atlantic Ocean and the Irish Sea. The Gulf Stream washes over our western shores, bringing with it a vast array of marine life. For beginners, the prospect of tackling these wild waters can be intimidating, but the rewards are incredible. From the silver flash of a summer mackerel to the heavy, head-shaking pull of an autumn bass, Irish waters are teeming with accessible fish.

In this comprehensive beginner’s guide, we will walk you through exactly what you need to start sea fishing in Ireland, where to go, what you can expect to catch, and how to stay safe while doing it. We’ll skip the complex jargon and focus on getting you your first bite.

1. Choosing the Right Gear (Without Breaking the Bank)

The first mistake beginners make is walking into a tackle shop and buying gear that is either far too heavy for shore fishing or heavily over-complicated. For shore, rock, and pier fishing in Ireland, you generally only need a solid, reliable spinning setup to get started.

  • The Rod: An 8ft to 10ft (2.4m to 3.0m) spinning rod is the perfect all-rounder. It provides enough length to cast over rocks and pier edges, keeping your line clear of snags, but is light enough to hold for hours without fatigue. Look for a "casting weight" of 20g to 60g.
  • The Reel: A 4000 or 5000 size spinning reel. Saltwater wreaks havoc on cheap, unsealed reels, so invest in one with good anti-corrosion properties from reputable brands like Penn, Shimano, or Daiwa. You don't need to spend hundreds; a €50-€80 reel will last years if rinsed with fresh water after every use.
  • The Line: While braided line offers incredible sensitivity and casting distance, it can fray easily when rubbed against sharp barnacles or rocks. As a beginner, spool your reel with a high-quality 15lb to 20lb monofilament line. It has built-in stretch, which acts as a shock absorber when fighting a fish, and offers excellent abrasion resistance.

2. What Will You Catch? (Top Target Species)

Depending on the season, the Irish coast offers a massive variety of species. Here are the top four for beginners, offering the best chance of success:

Mackerel (Summer / Autumn)

The undisputed king of beginner fish. Mackerel arrive in massive shoals during the warmer months, chasing sprats and sandeels. You don't even need bait—just a string of shiny feathers cast out and retrieved with a sink-and-draw jigging motion. When a shoal moves through, you can catch three or four on a single cast. They fight incredibly hard for their size and are unmatched on the BBQ when cooked fresh.

Pollock (Year-round)

Pollock love deep, kelpy water near rocky outcrops and cliffs. They are aggressive predators and will readily take silver spinners, dexter wedges, or soft plastic weedless lures retrieved slowly near the bottom. The initial dive of a big Pollock is legendary—they will instantly head straight down into the kelp, testing your drag system to its limits.

European Sea Bass (Summer / Autumn)

The prized, silver-scaled catch of Irish shore anglers. Bass hunt in the chaotic surf of sandy beaches and the shallow, rocky gullies of the South and West coasts (particularly Kerry, Cork, Waterford, and Wexford). They require a bit more stealth and skill to catch, but targeting them with shallow-diving hard lures or peeler crab baits in the dark is highly addictive.

Wrasse (Summer / Autumn)

Often overlooked, the Ballan Wrasse is one of Ireland's most colorful fish, looking more like a tropical reef species. They live deep in the rock crevices and kelp forests. Dropping a simple ragworm or soft plastic crab imitation straight down a rock face is a surefire way to hook these incredibly strong, dirty fighters.

Pro Tip: Always check the current Inland Fisheries Ireland (IFI) regulations for Bass fishing. There are strict catch-and-release periods (often early in the year) and strict bag limits (usually 2 per day) enforced throughout the year to protect dwindling stocks.

3. The Importance of Tides

The ocean is dictated by the moon and the tides. In Ireland, the sea level can rise and fall drastically (up to 5 or 6 meters in some bays) twice a day. As a general rule, the best shore fishing is almost always found during the two hours leading up to high tide and the hour just after high tide.

Why? Because moving water stirs up the seabed, dislodging crabs, worms, and small baitfish from their hiding places. This sudden abundance of food triggers larger predatory fish to move inshore and feed aggressively. When the tide goes slack (stops moving completely at peak high or low), the bites often dry up completely.

This is exactly why we built the Irish Fishing Hub app. Before you leave your house, check the live tide charts for your local station. Time your session to coincide with an incoming (flood) tide for the best results.

4. Essential Baits for Beginners

While lures are incredibly fun, sometimes the fish are sluggish and only want the real thing. Here are the most reliable baits to start with if you are bottom fishing:

  • Lugworm & Ragworm: The bread and butter of Irish sea fishing. Excellent for flatfish, wrasse, whiting, and codling. You can dig them yourself at low tide on muddy beaches or buy them fresh at local tackle shops.
  • Mackerel Strip: A sliver of fresh or frozen mackerel is oily, smelly, and irresistible to almost everything in the sea, especially dogfish, whiting, ray, and pollock.
  • Peeler Crab: The ultimate premium bait for Bass, Smooth-hound, and large Flounder. They are harder to find (you have to forage for them under rocks in estuaries) and keep alive, but they emit a scent trail that fish will travel hundreds of yards to find.
  • Squid: Tough, durable, and highly visible. A strip of squid tipped with a lugworm is a classic winter cod bait.

5. Safety First on the Irish Coast

The Atlantic Ocean is completely unforgiving. Every year, experienced anglers get into serious trouble on the Irish coast. No fish is worth your life. Always follow these golden rules:

  • Never turn your back to the sea: Freak rogue waves can sweep over rocks that look perfectly dry and safe. Always keep one eye on the swell.
  • Wear a lifejacket: If you are rock hopping, a slimline auto-inflate lifejacket is essential. It won't restrict your casting and could save your life.
  • Check the swell forecast: A sunny, windless day does not mean a calm sea. Offshore storms hundreds of miles away can send massive, deadly swells crashing onto the Irish coast. Use the swell charts in the Irish Fishing Hub app.
  • Tell someone where you are going: Always let a friend or family member know your exact location and expected return time. Phone signal is notoriously bad at the bottom of sea cliffs.
  • Wear proper footwear: Seaweed-covered rocks are like ice. Studded wading boots or specifically designed rock fishing cleats are highly recommended.

Conclusion

Sea fishing in Ireland is a lifelong journey of learning, adapting, and appreciating our incredible marine environment. Start simple: grab a spinning rod, a handful of lures or feathers, and head to your local pier or rock mark on an incoming tide. Don't overcomplicate your rigs.
