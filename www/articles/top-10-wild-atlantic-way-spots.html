Top 10 Fishing Spots on the Wild Atlantic Way

By Killian, Irish Fishing Hub • April 14, 2026 • 7 Min Read

Stretching 2,500km along Ireland’s western seaboard, the Wild Atlantic Way is not just a scenic driving route—it is arguably the greatest, most rugged, and most diverse saltwater fishery in Northern Europe. Battered by winter storms and bathed by the warm Gulf Stream in summer, these waters hold massive Pollock, hard-fighting wrasse, predatory Bass, and even Blue Shark.

Choosing where to fish along such a vast coastline can be overwhelming. To help you plan your next trip, we've compiled ten of the most productive and iconic shore fishing locations along the Wild Atlantic Way, working our way from South to North.

1. Beara Peninsula, Co. Cork

Target: Huge Pollock, Wrasse, Conger Eel

The Beara Peninsula is legendary among European anglers. The rock marks here drop off into incredibly deep water almost instantly. Deep spinning with heavy dexter wedges or soft plastics will yield massive Pollock in the 6lb to 10lb bracket. Bottom fishing with mackerel flappers at night offers a genuine chance at a 30lb+ Conger Eel. Extreme caution is required on these rocks.

2. Inch Beach, Co. Kerry

Target: Sea Bass, Flounder, Turbot

A massive, sprawling surf beach in Dingle Bay. Inch is famous for its surfcasting. When a good south-westerly wind kicks up a rolling surf, the Sea Bass move into the shallow gullies to hunt sandeels and crabs. Lugworm and peeler crab fished on a running ledger rig in the white water is highly effective here.

3. Brandon Creek, Co. Kerry

Target: Mackerel, Pollock, Ling

Nestled at the foot of Mount Brandon, this sheltered creek offers access to very deep water. It's a fantastic summer mark for filling a bucket with fresh mackerel. If you cast far out onto the clean bottom, you can pick up Dabs and Dogfish, while dropping straight down the harbour walls yields colourful Wrasse.

4. Black Head, Co. Clare

Target: Pollock, Wrasse, Mackerel

The lunar-like limestone landscape of the Burren meets the sea at Black Head. The limestone shelves drop away into the Atlantic, providing a perfect habitat for kelp beds. Float fishing with ragworm here is incredible for Ballan and Cuckoo Wrasse, while spinning produces endless sport with Pollock.

5. Fanore Beach, Co. Clare

Target: Sea Bass, Ray, Flatfish

Just down the road from Black Head, Fanore is a spectacular surf beach. It is renowned as one of the best Bass beaches in the Mid-West. In late summer and autumn, night fishing here with Sandeel or Squid baits can also tempt Small-Eyed Ray and Painted Ray into the shallows.

Safety Warning: The West Coast is notorious for rogue waves. Never fish rock marks on the Wild Atlantic Way during a heavy westerly swell. Always check the Live Swell charts in the Irish Fishing Hub app before departing.

6. Spiddal Pier, Co. Galway

Target: Conger, Ray, Dogfish, Mackerel

A very accessible and comfortable mark, perfect for beginners and families. High tide in the summer brings massive shoals of mackerel within casting distance of the pier. Fishing heavy gear off the end of the pier at night is a reliable tactic for Conger Eel and Huss.

7. Achill Island (Keem Bay), Co. Mayo

Target: Turbot, Sea Trout, Pollock

Keem Bay is visually stunning and equally productive. The clean, sandy bottom in the bay holds Turbot and Dabs. If you fish from the rocks on the southern edge of the bay, you can spin for Pollock. In late summer, there is always a chance of connecting with a Sea Trout spinning from the beach.

8. Mullaghmore Head, Co. Sligo

Target: Pollock, Wrasse, Coalfish

Famous worldwide for big wave surfing, Mullaghmore also offers stellar fishing on calmer days. The rocky outcrops are deep and kelpy. Soft plastic fishing for Pollock is highly effective here. Only fish these marks when the sea is exceptionally calm.

9. Killybegs Harbour, Co. Donegal

Target: Mullet, Flounder, Conger

Ireland's premier commercial fishing port is also a haven for shore anglers. The constant activity and fish processing draw massive shoals of Thick-Lipped Grey Mullet into the harbour. Fishing for Mullet with bread flake on light tackle is a highly specialized and thrilling challenge.

10. Malin Head, Co. Donegal

Target: Specimen Pollock, Wrasse, Tope (from boat)

The most northerly point of Ireland. The currents sweeping around Malin Head are ferocious, and the fishing is wild. Heavy spinning gear is required to haul massive Pollock up the rock faces. This is frontier fishing at its finest, offering a true test of an angler's skill and nerve.

Final Thoughts

The Wild Atlantic Way is vast, and these ten spots barely scratch the surface. The true joy of fishing the West Coast is exploring a random rocky headland on Google Maps, hiking down with a spinning rod, and discovering your own secret mark. Remember to respect the environment, take your litter home, and practice catch-and-release where possible.
