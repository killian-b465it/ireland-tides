Pike Fishing in Ireland: A Comprehensive Guide

By Killian, Irish Fishing Hub • April 14, 2026 • 7 Min Read

While Ireland is globally renowned for its Atlantic sea fishing and wild brown trout rivers, it is our Pike fishing that truly draws hardcore anglers from across Europe. The vast, limestone-fed loughs of the midlands and the sprawling Shannon and Erne river systems provide the perfect ecosystem for *Esox lucius* to grow to massive proportions. Fish over 20lbs are common, and "thirties" (fish over 30lbs) are caught every year.

Pike are the apex predators of Irish freshwaters. They are beautifully camouflaged, incredibly fast, and armed with rows of razor-sharp teeth. In this guide, we will cover the techniques, tackle, and unhooking procedures required to successfully target Irish Pike.

1. The Best Locations for Big Pike

Ireland has thousands of lakes (loughs), but if you want to target specimen-sized fish, you need to look at the larger, richer waters:

  • Lough Derg, Lough Ree, & Lough Allen: The three massive lakes on the River Shannon system. These act as inland seas and hold immense shoals of roach and bream, providing endless food for monster pike.
  • Lough Erne System (Cavan/Fermanagh): A complex maze of islands, bays, and deep channels. Excellent for boat fishing and trolling.
  • Lough Corrib & Lough Mask (Galway/Mayo): Traditionally famous for Trout, these gin-clear western lakes hold some of the most beautifully marked and largest Pike in the country.
  • The Grand and Royal Canals: If you don't have a boat, the canals running from Dublin to the midlands offer fantastic, highly accessible bank fishing with lures.

2. Lure Fishing Tactics

Spinning and lure fishing is the most active and exciting way to catch Pike. It allows you to cover vast amounts of water and triggers aggressive reaction strikes.

Top Lures for Irish Waters:

  • Soft Plastics (Shads): Large 6-inch to 9-inch rubber shads mimic the roach and bream that big pike feed on. Retrieve them slowly near the bottom with a steady sink-and-draw motion.
  • Jerkbaits: Hard, heavy lures that dart erratically from side to side when you twitch the rod. These are devastating in the autumn when water temperatures begin to drop.
  • Spoons and Spinners: The classic copper or silver spoon is still incredibly effective. The vibration of a size 5 Mepps spinner passing through a weed bed will often draw a pike out from cover.

3. Deadbaiting (The Winter Tactic)

During the cold winter months (November to March), a pike's metabolism slows down. They are less likely to chase a fast-moving lure and instead prefer an easy meal scavenged off the bottom. This is when deadbaiting shines.

Top Deadbaits:

  • Smelt: Smells heavily of cucumber, highly visible, and very tough on the hook.
  • Mackerel: Sea baits work incredibly well in freshwater. The high oil content of a mackerel tail creates a massive scent trail in the lake.
  • Roach & Lamprey: Natural baits that the pike are already accustomed to eating. Lamprey bleeds heavily, providing an incredible scent trail.

Deadbaits are usually fished on a ledger rig (weight on the bottom) or suspended under a highly visible pike float, rigged with a wire trace and twin treble hooks.

CRITICAL TACKLE RULE: You MUST use a wire trace when fishing for Pike. A pike's teeth will slice through heavy monofilament or braided line instantly. Always use a minimum 12-inch wire trace of at least 30lb breaking strain, regardless of whether you are lure fishing or deadbaiting.

4. Safe Unhooking and Fish Care

Pike may look terrifying, but they are actually very fragile fish and prone to stress, particularly in warmer summer waters. Proper fish care is mandatory.

  • Unhooking Mat: Never lay a pike on hard ground or gravel. Always use a padded, wet unhooking mat.
  • Long Forceps: You need 10-inch or 12-inch unhooking forceps (pliers) to safely reach inside the mouth and remove the hooks without getting your fingers near the teeth.
  • The Gill Grip: Learn the correct way to hold a pike. Gently slide your fingers under the gill plate and slide them forward to the chin. This naturally opens the mouth and gives you a firm, safe grip for unhooking.
  • Wire Cutters: Always carry heavy-duty wire cutters. If a hook is deeply embedded or awkward to reach, cut the hook rather than tearing the fish's flesh. Hooks are cheap; a 20lb pike takes a decade to grow.

5. When to Fish (The Seasons)

While Pike can be caught year-round, the approach changes. Spring (Post-Spawn) offers hungry fish eager to chase lures in the shallows. Summer requires fishing early mornings or late evenings to avoid the heat, focusing on deep drop-offs. Autumn is the prime feeding time when pike gorge themselves ahead of winter. Winter is the time for patience, deploying large deadbaits in deep holes.

Pike fishing is a highly rewarding discipline. Gear up properly, prioritize fish care, and get out on the Irish loughs. That massive strike could happen on your very next cast.
