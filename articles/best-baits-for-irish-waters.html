Top 5 Best Baits for Irish Waters

By Killian, Irish Fishing Hub • March 22, 2026 • 6 Min Read

Walk into any tackle shop in Ireland, and you'll be greeted by an overwhelming array of colorful lures, frozen packets, and live bait tanks. While modern lure fishing is incredibly effective and growing in popularity, traditional bait fishing remains the most reliable way to put fish on the shore. Fish rely heavily on scent in the murky, turbulent waters of the Irish coast, and nothing beats the real thing.

Whether you are fishing off a pier in Dublin, a storm beach in Kerry, or a deep rock mark in Donegal, your success heavily depends on matching the hatch. Here is a deep dive into the top 5 most effective baits for Irish waters, how to procure them, and what they catch.

1. Fresh Mackerel

If you could only choose one bait for the rest of your life in Ireland, it should be mackerel. It is the undisputed champion of the sea. Mackerel is incredibly oily, meaning it leeches a massive scent trail down-tide that can pull predatory fish from hundreds of yards away.

How to fish it:

  • Strips: A small pennel-rigged strip of the silver belly is devastating for Whiting, Pouting, and Pollock. The silver skin flashes in the current like an injured baitfish.
  • Chunks: A large, meaty chunk on a heavy hook is the go-to bait for Bull Huss, Conger Eel, and Ray.
  • Whole: Fished whole under a float or on a ledger rig, it is a premium bait for large Tope and predatory Pike (if used in freshwater).

Top Tip: Fresh is always best. Catch your own mackerel in the summer, vacuum seal them, and freeze them immediately. Repeatedly thawing and refreezing mackerel turns it to mush, making it impossible to keep on the hook.

2. Peeler Crab

Peeler crab is the holy grail of Irish shore fishing baits. A "peeler" is simply a common shore crab that is in the process of shedding its hard outer shell to grow a new one. During this vulnerable state, they emit a powerful pheromone that drives fish completely wild. For Bass and Smooth-hound, it is simply unmatched.

How to fish it:

You must peel the brittle outer shell off, revealing the soft, jelly-like crab underneath. Cut the crab in half, thread it onto a size 3/0 hook, and use elastic bait thread (bait floss) to bind it securely. Without elastic, the soft crab will fly off the hook during the cast.

Foraging Tip: You won't find peelers in most tackle shops because they don't last long. You must collect them yourself at low tide by overturning rocks in muddy estuaries and weed beds. Look for crabs hiding in pairs (a hard male protecting a peeling female). Always flip the rocks back exactly as you found them to protect the ecosystem!

3. Lugworm (Blow Lug & Black Lug)

Lugworm is the staple diet of almost every bottom-feeding fish in Ireland. If you are fishing a sandy or muddy beach for Flatfish (Flounder, Plaice, Dabs) or targeting winter Codling, lugworm is absolutely essential.

How to fish it:

Thread 2 or 3 worms up the hook and straight over the eye onto the line, creating a large, juicy sausage. For winter Cod, combining lugworm with a strip of squid (known as a "cocktail" bait) is a legendary tactic. The squid adds durability and visual attraction, while the lugworm provides the scent.

Blow Lug are the common yellowish/pink worms found by digging the squiggly casts on sandy beaches. They are watery and best used fresh. Black Lug are found deeper down the beach near the low water mark. They are much tougher, smell stronger, and can be frozen successfully.

4. Ragworm (King Rag)

Ragworm are aggressive, predatory worms that live in muddy estuaries and rocky kelp beds. They are highly active on the hook, wriggling frantically, which acts as a massive visual trigger for fish like Wrasse, Pollock, and Bass.

How to fish it:

Unlike lugworm, which you bunch up, a large King Ragworm should be hooked through the head (watch out for the pincers, they can nip!) and allowed to dangle naturally. They are fantastic when float fished over kelp beds for Wrasse or slow-retrieved behind a bombarda float for Bass.

5. Sandeel

Sandeels are a primary food source for fast, pelagic predators and flatfish alike. During the summer, huge shoals of sandeels bury themselves in the sand at low tide and emerge to feed when the water rises. Bass, Sea Trout, Pollock, and Rays gorge on them.

How to fish it:

Frozen sandeel is widely available in tackle shops. To rig them, pass the hook through the gills and out the belly, then secure the head to the eye of the hook with bait elastic. A whole sandeel fished on a running ledger rig over a clean sandy beach in the surf is a phenomenal tactic for Sea Bass and Ray.

Conclusion

While bait selection is crucial, presentation is equally important. Always use fresh bait when possible, ensure your hooks are razor-sharp, and use bait elastic to secure soft baits for maximum casting distance.
